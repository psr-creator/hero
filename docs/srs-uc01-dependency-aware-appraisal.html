01Why one use case, not the whole platform

The client's brief covers six capability areas. Building all six before a signature is how prototypes die. This specification instead isolates the one thread that proves all six at once — and that no competitor in this bracket demonstrates convincingly.

That thread is the client's own example: an HR Executive who must deliver attendance data, and a Payroll Manager whose KPI cannot be met if that data is late. Following a single dependent KPI pair through one complete cycle necessarily exercises the cascade, the workflows, the scoring scale, the ERP pull and the dashboards. Everything else in the platform is breadth on top of a mechanism this use case already proves.

Demo thesis

Every appraisal tool can score an individual. This one can tell you whose failure caused the score — and move the accountability to the right person, with an audit trail the HOD approves.

02Scope of UC-01

Reference scenario: X Contracting LLC, a subsidiary of Group X. Cycle H1-2026 (Jan–Jun), reviewed in June. Department: HR & Payroll. Two employees, one dependency link, one ERP connection.

In scope for the prototype

  • Four-level goal cascade with weight validation
  • KPI definition, approval and employee acknowledgment workflows
  • Scheduled ERP pull of attendance and payroll actuals
  • 1–5 band scoring with weighted roll-up
  • One-hop dependency resolution with HOD approval
  • Company / department / individual dashboards
  • Immutable audit trail on every score change

Deliberately out — phase 2

  • 360° and peer feedback
  • Competency / behavioural frameworks
  • Bell-curve normalisation and forced ranking
  • Cross-entity and multi-hop dependency chains
  • Increment, bonus and succession modelling
  • Arabic UI localisation
  • Mobile app (responsive web only in phase 1)

04Goal cascade model

Targets descend through four levels. Each level consumes a weight allocation from its parent, and the system refuses to advance a scorecard whose child weights do not total exactly 100%. This is what makes the cascade an accounting structure rather than a list of aspirations.

05Master process flow

The complete lifecycle of one cycle, by responsible actor. Six phases run left to right; the two vertical crossings at either end are deliberate — the cycle is opened by a human and closed by the system.

06KPI workflow states

A KPI is a controlled document. Once acknowledged it cannot be edited silently — a mid-cycle change creates a new version and re-enters approval, and both versions survive in the audit trail. This is the feature that protects the employer in a labour dispute and the employee against a moving target.

07ERP data capture

The platform never writes to the ERP. It reads on a schedule, maps ERP metric codes to KPI identifiers through a tenant-level mapping table, and stores every actual as an append-only record carrying its source reference. A correction from the ERP does not overwrite — it supersedes, and both rows remain visible.

08Scoring model

Achievement is normalised against target before banding, so KPIs measured in dirhams, days and percentages can sit on one scorecard and roll up honestly.

achievement_pct = actual ÷ target × 100   — when higher is better
achievement_pct = target ÷ actual × 100   — when lower is better (cost, defects, days)
weighted_score  = Σ ( bandᵢ × weightᵢ ) ÷ Σ weightᵢ   — weights total 100
BandLabelAchievementMeaning to the business
5Exceptional≥ 110%Materially beyond target; candidate for stretch objectives next cycle
4Exceeds100 – 109.9%Target met with margin
3Meets90 – 99.9%Delivered as agreed
2Partially meets75 – 89.9%Shortfall requiring a documented action plan
1Below expectation< 75%Formal performance improvement plan triggered

The same five thresholds convert the weighted score into a final appraisal band: 4.50–5.00 → 5, 3.50–4.49 → 4, 2.50–3.49 → 3, 1.50–2.49 → 2, 1.00–1.49 → 1. Bands and thresholds are tenant configuration, not code, so a client running a 1–4 or 1–10 scale is a settings change rather than a new build.

09Interdependency engine

This is the mechanism the rest of the platform exists to serve. A dependency link is declared at KPI definition time, approved with the KPI set, and evaluated automatically after scoring — but it never changes a rating on its own. It produces a proposal that a human approves, because an automatic score change nobody signed is worthless the moment it is challenged.

Design decision worth defending in the demo

The engine credits the consumer and flags the provider — it does not deduct from the provider twice. The HR Executive already scores 1 on their own late-delivery KPI; taking a further deduction for the downstream damage would punish one failure twice. Instead the provider's scorecard carries a visible downstream impact count, which is what the HOD actually needs to see when deciding where the real problem sits.

The slide that closes the deal

Same person. Same six months of actual output. Without dependency mapping the Payroll Manager scores 2.95 — "Meets expectations". With it he scores 3.65 — "Exceeds expectations", and the HR Executive's scorecard now carries two recorded downstream failures.

That gap is not a rounding artefact. It is the difference between an appraisal the manager argues with and one he signs — and between a root cause the HOD can see and one buried in a spreadsheet.

11Business rules

BR-01
Child weights on any scorecard must total exactly 100. Approval is blocked otherwise, with the shortfall shown.
BR-02
An individual scorecard carries a minimum of 4 and a maximum of 8 KPIs.
BR-03
A KPI cannot be edited after employee acknowledgment. A mid-cycle change creates version n+1 and re-enters approval and acknowledgment; all versions are retained.
BR-04
Employee acknowledgment is mandatory before scoring begins. Unacknowledged KPIs escalate to the HOD after 7 calendar days.
BR-05
A dependency link may only join two KPIs within the same cycle and the same legal entity. Cross-entity links are phase 2.
BR-06
Dependency resolution is single-hop. If A feeds B and B feeds C, only the directly declared link is evaluated — no transitive chains in phase 1.
BR-07
An adjustment may raise a consumer band by at most 2, and never above 5.
BR-08
An adjustment never reduces the provider's band. It writes a downstream-impact flag visible on the provider's individual and department scorecards.
BR-09
Every post-scoring rating change requires an approver identity, a reason of at least 20 characters, and a timestamp. No exceptions, including for administrators.
BR-10
ERP-sourced actuals are immutable. Corrections arrive as new records that supersede, and both remain queryable.
BR-11
An appraisal locks on employee acknowledgment. A dispute opens a review task and is recorded on the appraisal; it does not unlock the score.
BR-12
Cycles are fixed: H1 runs Jan 1 – Jun 30 with a review window of Jun 15 – Jul 15; H2 runs Jul 1 – Dec 31 with a review window of Dec 15 – Jan 15.
BR-13
A cycle cannot be locked while any scored KPI has an unresolved ERP sync failure or an undecided impact note.
BR-14
An employee's own manager cannot approve their own dependency adjustment where they are also the provider's manager; approval escalates one level.

12Functional requirements

Cascade and setup

FR-01Entity hierarchyCreate and maintain a tree of legal entities, departments and projects under one tenant, with unlimited depth and a display of each node's weight share.
FR-02Cycle managementOpen, freeze, reopen and lock bi-annual cycles per tenant, with configurable review windows and automatic status transitions.
FR-03Goal cascadeAllocate a parent objective across children by weight, with live validation that the allocation totals 100 before the scorecard can be submitted.

KPI lifecycle

FR-04KPI definitionCapture title, measure, unit, direction, target, weight, data source and evidence requirement, from a library template or freehand.
FR-05Dependency declarationDeclare a link from any KPI to another in the same cycle and entity, specifying type, alpha and impact window, subject to both managers' agreement at approval.
FR-06Approval workflowRoute a KPI set to the HOD with approve, reject-with-comment and return-for-rework outcomes, with notification and a visible SLA.
FR-07Employee acknowledgmentPresent the approved KPI set for e-signature acknowledgment, with a query path back to the manager and automatic escalation after 7 days.
FR-08VersioningRetain every version of a KPI with its approver and acknowledgment record; render a side-by-side diff on any mid-cycle revision.

Data and scoring

FR-09ERP ingestionPull actuals on schedule from a configured ERP endpoint or view, map metric codes to KPIs per tenant, and write append-only actual records with source references.
FR-10Manual captureAllow evidence-backed manual entry for KPIs with no ERP source, flagged distinctly everywhere the value appears.
FR-11Scoring engineCompute achievement against target by direction, apply the configured band scale, and roll weighted scores up the cascade to department, entity and group.
FR-12Dependency resolutionMatch provider breaches to consumer failures inside the impact window, compute attributable events, and generate a Dependency Impact Note for approval.
FR-13Adjustment approvalPresent the impact note with raw score, proposed score and the matched evidence; record the approve or reject decision with a mandatory reason.

Appraisal and visibility

FR-14Appraisal generationAssemble the final appraisal from scored KPIs, adjustments, manager narrative and the employee's self-comments, ready for the review meeting.
FR-15Final acknowledgmentCapture employee acknowledgment or dispute with comments; lock the record on acknowledgment and open a review task on dispute.
FR-16DashboardsThree levels — group and entity roll-up, department scorecard with dependency hot-spots, and individual progress against target with trend across cycles.
FR-17Dependency mapVisualise declared links across a department, showing which roles are upstream bottlenecks by count of downstream impacts in the cycle.
FR-18Audit trailRecord every state transition, score change and approval as an immutable event with actor, before, after, reason and timestamp; exportable per employee per cycle.

14Platform and non-functional requirements

The commercial model is subscription, so the build has to be a product from the first line. Three decisions carry that:

  1. Single application, tenant-isolated dataOne deployment serves every client. Every table carries tenant_id and every query is scoped at the data layer, not in application code. One client's cost of onboarding is a configuration record, not an environment.
  2. Configuration over customisationBand scales, cycle dates, weight limits, escalation SLAs, alpha defaults and the ERP field mapping are all tenant settings. A second client with a 1–10 scale and quarterly cycles is a form, not a branch.
  3. Connector pattern for ERPOne connector interface with per-ERP adapters. The first adapter is written for this client's ERP; the second client reuses everything above the adapter line.

15Demo storyboard

Twelve minutes, built around one narrative. Resist the urge to show breadth — the prototype wins on the thing nobody else shows.

  1. Open on the group dashboard — 60sOperational efficiency at 15% target, X Contracting tracking behind. Click through to HR & Payroll. Say nothing about features yet.
  2. Show the cascade — 90sWalk down the four levels. Try to submit with weights at 95% and let the validator refuse it. That one refusal establishes the system is an accounting structure, not a form.
  3. The KPI pair and the link — 2 minOpen the Payroll Manager's KPI-PR-01 and show the declared dependency on KPI-HR-01, with alpha and impact window agreed at approval. This is the first thing they have not seen elsewhere.
  4. Workflows in 60 secondsKPI set awaiting HOD approval, employee acknowledgment e-signed, one mid-cycle revision showing the version diff. Move fast — these are table stakes and they know it.
  5. Pull the ERP data live — 90sTrigger an on-demand sync. Six months of attendance and payroll timestamps land in the actuals table with source references. Nobody typed anything.
  6. Run scoring, show the raw result — 60sPayroll Manager at 2.95, "Meets expectations". Pause on it. Ask whether that is the right answer.
  7. Resolve the dependency — 3 minOpen the impact note. February and May, matched automatically, two days apart, inside the window. Show attributable = 1.60, the recomputed 93.3%, the proposed 1 → 3. Approve it as the HOD with a reason. Watch the score move to 3.65.
  8. Turn to the other side — 60sOpen the HR Executive's scorecard. Two downstream failures recorded. Then the department dependency map, with HR sitting as the upstream bottleneck. This is the moment the HOD in the room understands what they are buying.
  9. Close on the audit trail — 60sEvery transition, every approval, every reason, every timestamp. Then stop talking.
Questions to expect, and the honest answers

"Who decides alpha?" — Both managers, at KPI approval, before the cycle starts. Never retrospectively. That is a rule in the system, not a policy in a document.

"Can a manager game this?" — The link must be declared before the cycle and approved by the HOD, the evidence comes from ERP timestamps rather than claims, and the uplift is capped at two bands. Gaming it requires forging the ERP.

"What if the dependency chain is longer?" — Phase 1 resolves one hop. Say so plainly; multi-hop is a real design problem and pretending otherwise costs credibility in the room.
