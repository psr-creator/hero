Where the stock goes

Purchases are recorded. Sales are recorded. What happens in between is not.

The count at month end tells you what is left. It cannot tell you why. Without a record of what left the store and what the kitchen consumed, the difference has nowhere to be explained.

Expiry

The stock that expires is always the stock at the back.

Business Central captures batch and expiry on the goods receipt, picks by first expiry first out, and reports what is due to expire while there is still time to use it, discount it or return it.
Reverse calculation from POS

The POS tells you what was sold. The recipe tells you what that should have used.

Figures shown for illustration at AED 14 per kg. Business Central explodes the day's POS sales file through the recipe to get theoretical consumption, compares it against what the store actually issued, and reports the difference by item, by branch and by day. That difference is your wastage and utilisation number, and it is available the next morning rather than at the month end.

We mapped your operation end to end with your team — branches, main store, head office procurement, vendors, the main kitchen and the POS. The gaps in that map are the scope of the project.

Basic operations flow as mapped for Aaraamam. Click to enlarge. Three rules hold it together: every requirement is raised as a request and routed, never phoned; every transfer is issued by one location and receipted by the other; every sale is costed back through the recipe at the end of the day.
What the system does

The same eight points, handled.

01
Stock as a balanceEvery movement posts. On hand is a ledger you can trust between counts, not the count itself.
02
Batch and expiry at receiptDates captured on the goods receipt, picking by first expiry, and a report before the loss instead of after.
03
Transfer with a receipt legIssued by the store, received by the kitchen or the branch. What is in transit is visible, and shortages surface the same day.
04
Recipes with yieldPortion cost held in the system, and every dish re-costed automatically when a purchase price moves.
05
Consumption from the POS fileThe day's sales explode through the recipe into theoretical usage, per item and per branch.
06
Wastage as a transactionWaste, staff meals and returns posted in their own right, so they are counted rather than absorbed.
07
Requisition to invoiceRequest, RFQ, purchase order, goods receipt and invoice match, with price history by item and by vendor.
08
Close on the ledgerFood cost, margin and branch profitability from posted entries, not from a spreadsheet rebuilt each month.
