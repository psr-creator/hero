Where a POS was never meant to help

Eight things that happen behind the till.

OrderingRequests arrive all day. There is no cut-off to buy against.
ReceivingThe delivery note is believed. The scale is not consulted.
RejectionsShort or poor quality goods still land in stock.
TransfersMaterial leaves the store on a phone call.
Outlet to outletBorrowed items never come back on paper.
Batch and expiryNothing carries the date it arrived.
WasteFood that is thrown away has nowhere to be recorded.
ConsumptionWhat the kitchen used is never compared to what it sold.
Receiving

You are counting the delivery note, not the delivery.

Figures shown for illustration. Business Central posts the receipt against the purchase order at the accepted quantity, holds the short and rejected quantity open for a credit note or replacement, and matches the supplier invoice to what was accepted rather than what was claimed. Receiving variance by vendor becomes a report in its own right.
Expiry

The stock that expires is always the stock at the back.

Business Central captures batch and expiry on the goods receipt, picks by first expiry first out, and reports what is due to expire while there is still time to use it, discount it or return it.
Waste

The goal is not a gap of zero. It is a gap you can name.

Figures shown for illustration. Waste, over-preparation, staff meals, customer returns and dropped items are each posted as their own transaction with a reason code, against the item and the outlet. What is left once they are accounted for is the number worth investigating, and it is small enough to investigate.
Reverse calculation from POS

The POS tells you what was sold. The recipe tells you what that should have used.

Figures shown for illustration at AED 14 per kg. Business Central explodes the day's POS sales file through the recipe to get theoretical consumption, compares it against what the store actually issued, and reports the difference by item, by branch and by day. That difference is your wastage and utilisation number, and it is available the next morning rather than at the month end.
Between outlets

When one branch borrows from another, it is a loan, not a transfer.

Branch to branch transfer orders are standard in Business Central. What we add is the settlement view: the borrowing stays on an open list until the return is receipted, so the lending branch is never left explaining a shortfall it did not cause, and the borrowing branch has the return sitting on its next order.
Consulting and process design

Before the software, the process.

We mapped your operation end to end with your team — branches, main store, head office procurement, vendors, the main kitchen and the POS. That map is the backbone. The four things it does not yet carry are where the losses are.

Basic operations flow as mapped for Aaraamam. Click to enlarge. The four additions we propose: receive at the accepted quantity rather than the delivery note; give every transfer a receipt leg, including outlet to outlet; record waste with a reason; and reverse-calculate consumption from the day's POS file.
What the system does

The same eight points, handled.

01
A daily ordering cut-offOne cut-off, one consolidated order per vendor per day, placed against agreed lead times instead of trickling out all afternoon.
02
Receipt at the accepted quantityWeighed and checked at the door. Only what is accepted enters stock, and the invoice matches that figure.
03
Rejections held, not absorbedShort and rejected quantities stay open against the purchase order and drive the credit note or the replacement.
04
Transfer with a receipt legIssued by one location, received by the other. What is in transit is visible, and shortages surface the same day.
05
Outlet to outlet as a loanEmergency movements are recorded with a return expected, and stay on an open list until they are settled.
06
Batch and expiry at receiptDates captured on the goods receipt, picking by first expiry, and a report before the loss instead of after.
07
Waste with a reasonSpoilage, over-preparation, staff meals and returns posted separately, so the gap can be named rather than argued over.
08
Consumption from the POS fileThe day's sales explode through the recipe into theoretical usage, compared against what the store actually issued.
