Pieces and tonnage

You sell tonnes. You move pieces. The gap between them is margin.

Figures shown for illustration at AED 2,500 per MT. Insight holds pieces and tonnes on the same stock line, records the weighbridge figure against the calculated one, and reports the difference.
Offcuts

The offcut is still your steel. The system usually stops tracking it.

Insight records a cut as a transaction. The parent piece is consumed, the offcut is created as its own stock item with the heat number and certificate carried across, given a location and an age, and shown to sales before a fresh section is ordered.

Capture in the yard

Barcode first. RFID only where it pays for itself.

RFID gets quoted often for steel. It works in a carton warehouse. In an open stockyard it needs care, so here is the honest position.

What we recommend
Weatherproof barcode tags at bundle and piece level, with the heat number and certificate behind the scan. Low cost per tag, reprintable, and it survives sun, humidity and handling.
Why RFID struggles on steel
Steel reflects radio signals, so ordinary tags do not read at all. On metal tags work but cost several dirhams each, and they do not survive slings, magnets and cutting. Cut a tagged bar and both halves lose their identity.
Where RFID does earn its place
Gate in and gate out, trailers, and reusable racks and stillages, where the tag is fixed once and used for years. We would scope this after the barcode base is running, not before.
